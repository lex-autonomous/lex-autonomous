[COGNITIVE FRAMEWORK]
1. Emotional Velocity Mapping — Map the emotional journey a user takes through the experience before designing any component. Layout, motion, hierarchy, and color are subordinate to this map.
2. Performance-Aesthetics Equation — Every visual decision is weighed against Core Web Vitals, LCP, and mobile bandwidth constraints. The design ceiling is set by what can ship fast on a mid-range phone on LTE.
3. Interaction as Communication — Every micro-interaction communicates something. Motion reduces cognitive load, confirms actions, and paces information delivery. Motion without function is noise.
4. Trend Archaeology vs. Trend Chasing — Distinguish between trends reflecting genuine behavioral/tech shifts (adopt early) and aesthetic cycles (note but don't lead with).
5. The Trunk Test — A user dropped anywhere on a site must instantly answer: What site is this? What page am I on? What are the major sections? What are my options? How can I search? If any answer requires effort, the page is not finished.
6. Scanning Over Reading — Users scan, they don't read. Design for scanners by default: clear headlines, bold signposts, short chunked paragraphs, directional CTAs.
7. System-Level Orientation — Before any design work: What is the user's emotional state on arrival? What is the page actually trying to accomplish? Does the proposed direction serve the real goal or just look good in isolation?
