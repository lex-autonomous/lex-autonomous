### 4a. Platform facts
- Pricing page: Hobby $9/month with a 10% transaction fee. Pro $99/month with a 2.9% transaction fee. Both include unlimited members, courses, video, live calls, a custom URL, and affiliates. 14-day free trial listed. [verified, skool.com/pricing, fetched 2026-10-28; verify again before buying]

- Platform limits per the same review: no brand colors or fonts, only a one-click email broadcast (no sequences), no funnel builder, no quizzes beyond polls, member-only data export (not course content), and discoverability depends on Discovery ranking. [reported]
- Implication: lead capture, tagging, email sequences, and quizzes (the "intake quiz" in Reid's design) sit outside Skool, in GHL via Nadia. [assumption]
- Skool was founded by Sam Ovens (2019). Alex Hormozi joined as partner and investor in 2024; Acquisition.com runs Skool Games. [reported, tools4skool.com]

### 4b. What the market looks like
Sample: 1,000 English-language Trending Discovery listings, September 5, 2026. [reported, revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/statistics] It is a sample, not the platform.
- Median community size: 200 members. 72.9% have under 500. Only 30 of 1,000 have 5,000+.
- 95.9% are paid; 86.8% bill monthly.
- Monthly price median: $39. Largest band: $50–$99.99 (29.3%).
- Revenue and churn: not reported by the source.

Examples (Oct 24, 2026 snapshot, revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/best-communities) [reported]:
- Clief Notes — AI foundations, free tier with 40+ lessons, paid at $11 and $97, ~53,600 members. Lesson: a deep free tier can carry a community.
- Maker School (Nick Saraev) — AI automation, $184/month, ~2,700 members, 218 videos and guides, weekly calls, a 90-day refund promise tied to a first client. Lesson: a narrow promise, live cadence, and a clear refund rule. His "$300K/month" figure appeared in a headline but I couldn't verify it. [unverified]
- Ben Heath Meta Ads Academy — $59/month, ~1,300 members.
- Matt Ryder, sales training — $69/month, ~1,600 members.
- AI Video Bootcamp — $9/month, ~30,000 members. Lesson: low price and huge volume is a different model from ours.

### 4c. The credit niche on Skool
Quick scan of four public listings, 2026-10-28. [reported; small sample]
- The Credit Hub Elite: $67/month after a 7-day trial; 30–35 members; says its price rises to $97 at 50 members; offers "free done-for-you credit repair"; claims "$30M funded" and "1,000+ credit transformations" without evidence; a $6,076 "value stack."
- Ultimate Credit Academy: $27/month; 2 members; DIY dispute templates; positions against "$99/month gurus"; no credentials or refund terms listed.
- Also found, not reviewed: Easy Financial Credit Academy, Built To Last Credit Academy, RCD Smart Start Academy.
- Pattern: small communities, big claims, scarcity pricing, unverified numbers, no stated credentials, vague "done-for-you" services.
- Opening for us: calm, compliant, clearly credentialed, no pressure, honest about limits. [assumption]

### 4d. Playbook sources
One third-party guide (stickyhive.ai, "Skool Community Best Practices 2026") supplies most of the numbers below. Its author states these come from experience, not independent data. [reported] Use them as starting targets and replace them with our own numbers by day 60.

**Posting cadence:** 0–50 members, 3–4 posts a week, mostly from us. 50–150, about one a day. 150–500, one or two a day, mixed. 500+, mostly members. Don't post more than two a day ourselves early on.
**Naming:** 3–5 words, [audience] + [outcome], under 40 characters. Put audience and outcome in the first ~150 characters of the description (that's what shows in previews).
**Categories (under 100 members):** Announcements, Introductions, General Discussion, Wins & Progress, Resources.
**Classroom:** 4 achievement levels; end each lesson with a community action step.
**First 7 days:** Day 1, automated welcome DM, personal reply to the intro within 24 hours. Days 2–3, one quick win in 48–72 hours. Days 4–5, personal invite to the first live call. Days 6–7, connect them with 2–3 members.
**Live rhythm:** at least one 60-minute weekly call at the same time (about half teaching, half Q&A). Add a biweekly co-working hour and a monthly workshop or hot seat.
**Weekly rhythm:** Monday goals, Wednesday check-in, Friday wins, Sunday digest.
**Engagement:** reply to early comments within 60–120 minutes; tag 3–5 members on purpose; use prompts that take 15–30 seconds; run a 7-day mini challenge.
**Gamification:** Skool has 9 levels; rename them; turn it on from day one; use the 7-day, 30-day, and all-time leaderboards; tie unlocks to value; keep it winnable by recognizing climbers and first-time posters, not just the top names. Exact point values and thresholds are not documented in anything I could read, so test them in the live community. [reported]
**Benchmarks (targets, not facts):**
- Day 1 activation: 30%+ good, 50%+ excellent
- Day 7 retention: 40%+ good, 60%+ excellent
- Intro completion: 50%+ good, 70%+ excellent
- First live event attendance: 20%+ good, 40%+ excellent
- Day 30 retention: 30%+ good, 50%+ excellent
- Monthly active: 20%+ good, 40%+ excellent
- Monthly churn: under 10% good, under 5% excellent
- 30-day targets: 50–100 members, 10+ posts from members rather than us
**Paid-tier timing:** consider opening after 100+ engaged free members, with event attendance near 20% and monthly active near 40%. Annual pricing at 2–3 months free.
**Pricing guidance (third-party, promotional):** a narrow outcome with structured access at roughly $97–$297/month, or £9–£49 for a first price, is suggested by different sources. They conflict, and both assume an existing audience. [reported, weak] Our own competitor scan shows $27 and $67 in the credit niche.

### 4g. Credit law, as of research
- CROA (federal): no payment before services are fully performed; written disclosure before any contract; written contract with services, guarantees, and timeline; three-day right to cancel; non-compliant contracts are void; no deceptive claims; credit repair organizations can't remove accurate, current negative information; advising use of an EIN in place of an SSN can be a felony. [verified, ftc.gov/node/45184]
- Stout v. FreeScore, LLC (9th Cir., 2014): the court held a company was a "credit repair organization" even though it promised only scores and reports, because its marketing implied it would help improve credit, for money. It noted other courts reached the opposite result on pure information. [reported, Venable summary]
- California is in the Ninth Circuit and has its own credit services statute. I haven't read it. [open: counsel]
- None of this is legal advice. Treat it as the reason for the attorney review in section 3.

## 5. Phase plan (draft for Lex)

**Phase 0 — Foundation (before anything opens)**
Decide name, promise, and price strategy. Attorney review of offer structure and copy. Compliance pack: community rules, disclaimers, testimonial policy. Write About page, Start Here lesson, welcome DM, pinned posts. Reid delivers module 1 with a real first win. Nadia builds lead capture, tags, and an email path outside Skool. Create the group; set levels, categories, and join questions. Exit when: legal sign-off on copy, Start Here done, calendar for the first 4 weeks scheduled.

**Phase 1 — Founding cohort (free, 0–100 members, 30–60 days)**
Invite 20–50 hand-picked people from Lex's network and existing audiences. Personal welcome to every member. Weekly live call at a fixed time. Run one 7-day challenge. Collect honest feedback. Exit when (targets): Day 7 retention 40%+, 50+ members, 10+ member-written posts, event attendance 15–20%+.

**Phase 2 — Paid tier**
Open once Phase 1 targets are met and legal has signed off. Offer founding members a clear, honest price with no countdown. State refund and cancellation terms in plain words. Exit when: 30 days of retention data on paid members.

**Phase 3 — Scale and graduate**
Add content from Sheila and Kaiya. Build the graduation path to the next verticals. Consider affiliates (with Dex and counsel). Consider Skool Games only if Lex chooses to.
