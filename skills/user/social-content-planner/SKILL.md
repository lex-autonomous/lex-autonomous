# Social Content Planner

A structured operating framework for producing weekly content calendars and ready-to-publish copy for Lex Autonomous Facebook and Instagram accounts.

**Applies to:** Sheila Hartley and any content-producing agent working on Lex Autonomous social content.

---

## Step 1 — Brief Validation (Run Before Every Calendar)

Before writing a single post, validate the brief. A vague brief produces a generic week.

Run this check against the input provided:

**Required to proceed:**
- [ ] Is there a topic, theme, or focus for the week? (Can be a single sentence — "AI for small business" is enough)
- [ ] Is there anything happening this week that content should tie to? (Event, launch, announcement, seasonal moment)
- [ ] Is there a primary goal for the week? (Awareness, trust-building, lead generation, engagement)

**If any of these are missing**, ask the single most important clarifying question to resolve the biggest gap. One question only. Do not ask all three.

**If all three are present**, proceed directly to Step 2. Do not ask for more.

**Default assumptions (use if not specified):**
- Goal: trust-building and authority (this is the baseline for Lex Autonomous at current stage)
- Audience: small business owners, solopreneurs, and entrepreneurs curious about practical AI
- Tone: Lex's voice (see Step 3)

---

## Step 2 — Content Pillar Mapping

Every week should draw from the Lex Autonomous content pillar system. Not every pillar needs to appear every week — but the week should never be all one type.

### The Five Pillars

**1. Education**
Teach something practical about AI. Show how it works, what it does, why it matters for business owners. No fluff — specific, actionable, useful.
*Examples: "3 things AI actually does in a business" / "How to delegate to an AI agent" / "The real difference between automation and AI"*

**2. Proof / Reality Check**
Counter a common AI misconception. Show the gap between hype and reality. Position Lex Autonomous as the grounded, honest voice in a noisy space.
*Examples: "No, AI won't replace your team" / "What AI tools actually can't do" / "Why most AI implementations fail"*

**3. Behind the Build**
Show Lex Autonomous in motion. Agent builds, system updates, client work, tools being used. Builds credibility through transparency.
*Examples: Process clips, screenshots of work, "we just built X for Y" moments*

**4. Perspective / POV**
Lex's direct take on something in the AI space. An opinion, a trend observation, a clear stance. Not hot takes for engagement — grounded perspective from someone actually doing the work.
*Examples: "Here's what I think is overhyped in AI right now" / "The one thing most AI consultants miss"*

**5. Invitation**
Low-friction call to action. Not sales pressure — curiosity + access. Who is this for, what can they get, how do they start.
*Examples: DM, comment to get something, link to a resource, soft ask for discovery call*

### Weekly Pillar Mix Guidance

| Week Goal | Suggested Mix |
|---|---|
| Trust-building (default) | 2 Education + 1 Proof + 1 Behind the Build + 1 Perspective + 2 flex |
| Awareness / reach | 2 Education + 2 Proof + 1 POV + 2 flex |
| Lead generation | 2 Education + 1 POV + 2 Invitation + 2 flex |

Flex slots = repeat strongest pillar for the week, or tie to current event/announcement.

## Step 3 — Voice Enforcement

Every post must pass this check before it goes into the calendar.

### Lex Autonomous Voice Standard
- **Calm.** No urgency. No "you need this NOW." No countdown pressure.
- **Grounded.** Specific and real. No vague claims. If you say AI saves time, say where.
- **Direct.** No meandering. Say the thing. Then stop.
- **Plain English.** No jargon unless explained. No buzzwords used for effect.
- **No hype.** "Game-changing," "revolutionary," "transform your business overnight" — none of it.
- **No guru energy.** No quotes attributed to Lex with forced profundity. No "I used to struggle too" unless it's genuinely true and relevant.
- **Authority through usefulness.** The post earns trust by being genuinely helpful, not by performing expertise.

### Voice Audit — Run on Every Post

Before finalizing any post, check:
1. Does this sound like a real person talking, or like content marketing?
2. Would someone read this and think "that's useful" — or "that's trying to sell me something"?
3. Is there any word or phrase that would feel at home in a guru's Instagram? If yes, rewrite it.
4. Is the sentence structure direct — or does it build to a reveal for effect? Flatten it.

**If a post fails any of these, rewrite before including it in the calendar. Do not include a post that fails the voice check.**

## Step 4 — Platform-Native Sequencing

Facebook and Instagram are not the same channel. Assign platform before writing, then write to that platform's mechanics.

### Facebook (Lex Autonomous Business Page)

**Who's there:** Slightly older, more business-minded audience. Higher tolerance for depth. Comes to read, not just scroll visuals.

**What works:**
- Longer-form educational posts (200–400 words) that teach something in full
- POV posts with a clear, well-developed take
- Behind-the-build posts with context and narrative
- Posts that open a discussion or ask a question worth answering

**Format guidance:**
- Lead with a strong first sentence (this is what shows before "See more")
- No need for line-break formatting tricks — write in clean paragraphs
- Hashtags: 3–5 max, only if relevant; not required
- CTAs: soft and natural ("drop a comment," "feel free to DM me," "link in bio")

**Avoid on Facebook:**
- Pure image captions with no substance
- Overly short posts that feel like afterthoughts
- Hard sell language

---

### Instagram (Lex Autonomous Business Account)

**Who's there:** Visually-oriented, faster scroll. Arrives skeptical. Smaller window to earn attention.

**What works:**
- Short, punchy educational hooks that deliver the value immediately
- Proof/Reality Check posts that pattern-interrupt ("everyone says X, but actually...")
- Invitation posts with clear, low-friction asks
- Carousel-style thinking (even in captions: "3 things..." sets up a list structure)

**Format guidance:**
- First line must earn the stop — this is the only line visible before "More"
- Short paragraphs or single lines with breaks — visual breathing room matters
- Hashtags: 5–10 relevant tags in first comment or end of caption
- CTAs: specific and easy ("DM me 'AI' to get the guide," "comment YES if this resonates")

**Avoid on Instagram:**
- Dense paragraphs
- Posts that start with "I" (algorithm and perception both flag it)
- Long-winded context before the point

## Step 5 — Calendar Output Format

Deliver the complete 7-day calendar in this exact structure. One post per block. No summaries — full copy, ready to publish.

```
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
DAY: [Monday / Tuesday / etc.]
PLATFORM: [Facebook / Instagram / Both]
PILLAR: [Education / Proof / Behind the Build / Perspective / Invitation]
FORMAT: [Single image post / Carousel / Reel caption / Story / Link post]

COPY:
[Full post copy — written, not outlined. Ready to paste and publish.]

HASHTAGS: [If Instagram — list here. If Facebook or not needed — write "N/A"]
FORMAT NOTE: [One line on visual direction, if relevant]
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
```

Repeat this block for all 7 days.

### Weekly Rhythm Guidance

A strong week has variation in:
- **Depth** — not all long, not all short
- **Tone** — some practical/direct, some reflective/observational
- **Platform** — not everything on one channel
- **Ask level** — most posts give, one or two invite action

A flat week (all same length, all same structure, all same pillar) is a failure regardless of copy quality.

---

## Step 6 — Final Output Check

Before delivering the calendar, run this pass:

1. Does every post pass the voice audit? (Step 3)
2. Is there pillar variety across the 7 days? (No pillar appears more than 3 times unless the week goal calls for it)
3. Is platform assignment intentional — not just "post this everywhere"?
4. Is every post genuinely useful, or did any slot get filled with filler?
5. Would Lex read this week and feel it represents the brand accurately?

**If any post fails, replace it. Don't deliver a week with known weaknesses.**
